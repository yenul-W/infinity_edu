Part A

What is Direct Variation?

Definition — Direct Variation

Direct variation (also called direct proportion) is a special linear relationship where one quantity is always a constant multiple of another, and both quantities are zero at the same time. The equation is:

\[y = kx\]
  • \(k\) is the constant of variation — the ratio y/x, which stays the same for all data points.
  • \(x\) is the independent variable (what you choose or measure).
  • \(y\) is the dependent variable (what you calculate from x).

Key visual property: the graph is a straight line that passes through the origin \((0, 0)\). If the line does not go through the origin, it is not direct variation.

Real-world examples

Buying apples

Apples cost $2.10 per kg. Total cost \(C\) depends on weight \(w\).

\(k = 2.10\)

\(C = 2.10w\)

Wages

Paid $21/hr. Total earnings \(E\) depend on hours \(h\).

\(k = 21\)

\(E = 21h\)

Wheel revolutions

Each revolution covers 2.5 m. Distance \(D\) depends on revolutions \(R\).

\(k = 2.5\)

\(D = 2.5R\)

Notice that in every example: (i) both quantities start at zero together, and (ii) doubling x also doubles y. These are the hallmarks of direct variation.

Explore \(y = kx\) — change \(k\) and see what happens

y = 2x

When k is larger, the line is steeper. All lines pass through the origin — the defining property of direct variation.

Part B

Finding the Constant of Variation

Method — Finding k

To find \(k\), substitute any known \((x, y)\) pair (where \(x \neq 0\)) into \(y = kx\) and solve:

\[k = \frac{y}{x}\]

Once you have \(k\), write the equation \(y = kx\) and use it to find any other value.

Worked Example — From a word problem

I earned $282 for 12 hours of work. Earnings \(E\) vary directly with hours \(h\).

  1. Find \(k\):   \(k = \dfrac{E}{h} = \dfrac{282}{12} = 23.5\)
  2. Write the equation:   \(E = 23.5h\)
  3. Predict earnings for 15 hours:   \(E = 23.5 \times 15 = \$352.50\)

The value \(k = 23.5\) is the hourly rate — $23.50 per hour.

Worked Example — From a table

A table shows the cost of apples at different weights. Is this direct variation? If so, find k.

Weight (w kg) 1 2 3 4
Cost (C $) 2.10 4.20 6.30 8.40

Check: is C/w constant? → 2.10/1 = 2.10, 4.20/2 = 2.10, 6.30/3 = 2.10 ✓

Yes — direct variation with \(k = 2.10\). Equation: \(C = 2.10w\).

Worked Example — From a graph

A graph of direct variation passes through (4, 10). Find k and write the equation.

\(k = \dfrac{y}{x} = \dfrac{10}{4} = 2.5\)

Equation: \(y = 2.5x\). This can be verified: (0, 0) should also lie on the graph.
