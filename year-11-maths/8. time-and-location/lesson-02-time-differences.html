Theory

Converting Time Units

The base conversions between seconds, minutes and hours are:

  • \(1\) minute \(= 60\) seconds
  • \(1\) hour \(= 60\) minutes \(= 3600\) seconds

To convert to a smaller unit, multiply. To convert to a larger unit, divide.

Worked Example

Convert 2.5 hours into minutes, then into seconds.

\(2.5 \times 60 = 150\) minutes

\(150 \times 60 = 9000\) seconds

Worked Example

Convert 258 seconds into minutes and seconds.

\(258 \div 60 = 4\) remainder \(18\), so \(258\) seconds \(= 4\) minutes \(18\) seconds.

Theory

12-Hour and 24-Hour Time

12-hour time uses the numbers 12:00 to 11:59 twice each day, labelled am (midnight to just before noon) or pm (noon to just before midnight).

24-hour time runs from 0000 to 2359, with no am/pm label — hours are written as 4 digits (hhmm).

12-hour time24-hour time
12:00 am (midnight)0000
9:15 am0915
12:00 pm (noon)1200
3:40 pm1540
11:52 pm2352

Converting pm to 24-hour time: add 12 to the hour (except 12 pm, which stays 1200). Converting am to 24-hour time: keep the hour the same (except 12 am, which becomes 0000).

Worked Example

Convert 7:25 pm to 24-hour time, and convert 0640 to 12-hour time.

\(7\text{:}25\text{ pm} = 7 + 12 = 19\), so \(7\text{:}25\text{ pm} = 1925\).

\(0640 = 6\text{:}40\text{ am}\) (hour is less than 12, so it stays am).

Theory

Elapsed Time

To find elapsed time, subtract the start time from the finish time. It's often easiest in 24-hour time, breaking the calculation into hours and minutes separately, or counting forward in convenient steps (e.g. to the next hour, then in hours, then remaining minutes).

Worked Example

A train departs at 9:45 am and arrives at 1:20 pm. How long is the journey?

9:45 am to 12:00 pm \(= 2\) h \(15\) min

12:00 pm to 1:20 pm \(= 1\) h \(20\) min

Total \(= 2\text{h }15\text{min} + 1\text{h }20\text{min} = 3\text{h }35\text{min}\)

Worked Example

A flight leaves at 2340 and lands at 0615 the next day. Find the flight duration.

2340 to 0000 (midnight) \(= 20\) min

0000 to 0615 \(= 6\)h \(15\)min

Total \(= 20\text{min} + 6\text{h }15\text{min} = 6\text{h }35\text{min}\)

Theory

Time Zones and Daylight Saving

Australia has three main standard time zones:

  • Eastern Standard Time (AEST), UTC+10 — NSW, VIC, QLD, TAS, ACT
  • Central Standard Time (ACST), UTC+9:30 — SA, NT
  • Western Standard Time (AWST), UTC+8 — WA

During daylight saving (typically early October to early April), clocks in NSW, VIC, SA, TAS and ACT move forward 1 hour (AEDT = UTC+11, ACDT = UTC+10:30). QLD, NT and WA do not observe daylight saving.

Worked Example

It is 3:00 pm AEST in Sydney (not daylight saving). What time is it in Perth (AWST)?

AEST is UTC+10, AWST is UTC+8, a difference of \(2\) hours. Perth is behind Sydney, so it is \(3\text{:}00\text{ pm} - 2\text{h} = 1\text{:}00\text{ pm}\) in Perth.

Worked Example

During daylight saving, it is 6:00 pm AEDT in Sydney. What time is it in Brisbane (which does not observe daylight saving)?

AEDT is UTC+11, Brisbane stays on AEST (UTC+10), a difference of \(1\) hour. Brisbane is behind Sydney, so it is \(6\text{:}00\text{ pm} - 1\text{h} = 5\text{:}00\text{ pm}\) in Brisbane.

Theory

Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)

UTC is the global time standard, based on the Prime Meridian (0° longitude). Every location's local time can be written as an offset from UTC, e.g. Sydney (AEST) is UTC+10, meaning local time is 10 hours ahead of UTC.

To convert local time to UTC, subtract the offset. To convert UTC to local time, add the offset.

Worked Example

It is 8:00 pm AEST (UTC+10) in Sydney. What is the UTC time?

\(8\text{:}00\text{ pm} - 10\text{h} = 10\text{:}00\text{ am}\) UTC (same day).

Worked Example

UTC time is 22:00. What is the local time in Perth (AWST, UTC+8)?

\(22\text{:}00 + 8\text{h} = 30\text{:}00 = 06\text{:}00\) the next day, so it is 6:00 am the next day in Perth.
