Theory

Latitude and Longitude

Every point on Earth's surface can be described with two angles measured from the centre of the Earth: latitude and longitude.

Latitude

Latitude measures how far a point is north or south of the equator (0°). Lines of latitude are horizontal circles running parallel to the equator, called parallels. Latitude ranges from 0° at the equator to 90°N at the North Pole and 90°S at the South Pole.

Longitude

Longitude measures how far a point is east or west of the Prime Meridian (0°), which passes through Greenwich, England. Lines of longitude are called meridians — they run from pole to pole. Longitude ranges from 0° to 180°E and 0° to 180°W.

Position coordinates

A position is written as (latitude, longitude), each with a direction letter. For example, Sydney is located at approximately \(33.9°\text{S}, 151.2°\text{E}\).

CityLatitudeLongitude
Sydney, Australia33.9° S151.2° E
London, UK51.5° N0.1° W
Tokyo, Japan35.7° N139.7° E
Los Angeles, USA34.1° N118.2° W

Degrees can be split into minutes (1° = 60′) for greater precision, e.g. \(33°54'\text{S}\) means 33 degrees and 54 minutes south.

Worked Example

A ship is located at \(20°\text{S}, 60°\text{W}\). Describe its position in words.

The ship is 20 degrees south of the equator and 60 degrees west of the Prime Meridian — placing it in the South Atlantic Ocean, off the coast of South America.

Theory

Locating Positions

Without digital tools: use a printed map or globe with latitude/longitude grid lines. Find the horizontal line matching the latitude and the vertical line matching the longitude — the position is where they cross.

With digital tools: mapping apps (e.g. Google Maps) display coordinates directly, or let you search a place name to reveal its latitude and longitude. GPS devices report position as a live coordinate pair.

Worked Example

On a world map with grid lines every 10°, a city sits 3 lines above the equator and 4 lines to the right of the Prime Meridian. What are its approximate coordinates?

Latitude \(= 3 \times 10° = 30°\text{N}\); Longitude \(= 4 \times 10° = 40°\text{E}\). Approximate position: \(30°\text{N}, 40°\text{E}\).

When reading coordinates, always check the direction letters (N/S and E/W) — mixing these up places you on the opposite side of the world.

Theory

Longitude and Time Differences

Earth rotates 360° every 24 hours, so it turns \(360° \div 24 = 15°\) of longitude every hour. This gives the key rule:

Key Rule

\[ \text{Time difference (hours)} = \dfrac{\text{difference in longitude (°)}}{15} \]

Places east of another location are ahead in time. Places west are behind.

Worked Example

City A is at \(30°\text{E}\) and City B is at \(75°\text{E}\). Find the time difference and state which city is ahead.

Difference in longitude \(= 75° - 30° = 45°\)

Time difference \(= 45 \div 15 = 3\) hours

City B is further east, so City B is 3 hours ahead of City A.

Worked Example

Perth is at approximately \(116°\text{E}\) and Sydney is at approximately \(151°\text{E}\). If it is 2:00 pm in Perth, what time is it in Sydney (ignoring daylight saving)?

Difference in longitude \(= 151° - 116° = 35°\)

Time difference \(= 35 \div 15 = 2\tfrac{1}{3}\) hours \(\approx 2\) hours 20 minutes

Sydney is east of Perth, so Sydney is ahead: \(2\text{:}00\text{ pm} + 2\text{h }20\text{min} = 4\text{:}20\text{ pm}\).
