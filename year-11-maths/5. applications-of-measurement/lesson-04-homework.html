Question 1 Lesson 1

The length of a window is measured as 2.4 m. Which calculation gives the percentage error for this measurement?

A \(\dfrac{0.05}{2.4} \times 100\)
B \(\dfrac{0.05}{100} \times 2.4\)
C \(\dfrac{0.5}{2.4} \times 100\)
D \(\dfrac{0.5}{100} \times 2.4\)
Answer: A
Absolute error \(= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.1 = 0.05\) m (precision 0.1 m). \(\% \text{error} = \dfrac{\text{absolute error}}{\text{measurement}} \times 100 = \dfrac{0.05}{2.4} \times 100\).

Question 2 Lesson 1

What is 1 560 200 km written in standard form, correct to two significant figures?

A \(1.56 \times 10^4\) km
B \(1.56 \times 10^6\) km
C \(1.6 \times 10^4\) km
D \(1.6 \times 10^6\) km
Answer: D
\(1\,560\,200 = 1.5602 \times 10^6\), rounded to 2 sig. figs. \(\approx 1.6 \times 10^6\).

Question 3 Lesson 1

Peter rides his bike at a speed of 27 km/h. What is this speed in m/s?

A 7.5 m/s
B 18.75 m/s
C 97.2 m/s
D 450 m/s
Answer: A
\(27 \text{ km/h} = 27\,000 \text{ m} \div 3600 \text{ s} = 7.5\) m/s.

Question 4 Lesson 1

The mass of a grain of sand is 15 micrograms. What is this in kilograms?

A \(1.5 \times 10^{-7}\) kg
B \(1.5 \times 10^{-8}\) kg
C \(1.5 \times 10^{-9}\) kg
D \(1.5 \times 10^{-10}\) kg
Answer: B
\(1\) microgram \(= 10^{-6}\) g \(= 10^{-9}\) kg. \(15 \times 10^{-9} = 1.5 \times 10^{-8}\) kg.

Question 5 Lesson 2

What is the volume of a rectangular prism 30 cm × 5 cm × 0.05 m × 4 m long, in cubic centimetres? (Hint: 0.05 m = 5 cm, 4 m = 400 cm)

A 6 cm³
B 600 cm³
C 60 000 cm³
D 6 000 000 cm³
Answer: C
Convert all lengths to cm: \(30 \times 5 \times 400 = 60\,000\) cm³.

Question 6 Lesson 2

Two identical spheres (diameter 12 cm) fit exactly inside a cylindrical container. What is the volume of the container, to the nearest cm³?

A 1357 cm³
B 2714 cm³
C 5429 cm³
D 10 857 cm³
Answer: B
Radius = 6 cm, height = 2 × 12 = 24 cm. \(V = \pi r^2 h = \pi \times 6^2 \times 24 \approx 2714\) cm³.

Question 7 Lesson 2

The Louvre Pyramid has a square base with side length 35 m and perpendicular height 22 m. What is its volume, to the nearest m³?

A 257 m³
B 1027 m³
C 8983 m³
D 26 950 m³
Answer: C
\(V = \tfrac{1}{3}Ah = \tfrac{1}{3}(35^2)(22) = \tfrac{1}{3}(1225)(22) = 8983.3\) m³.

Question 8 Lesson 2

A path 1.5 m wide surrounds a circular lawn of radius 3 m. What is the approximate area of the path?

A 7.1 m²
B 21.2 m²
C 35.3 m²
D 56.5 m²
Answer: C
Outer radius = 4.5 m. \(A = \pi(4.5^2 - 3^2) = \pi(20.25-9) = \pi(11.25) \approx 35.3\) m².

Question 9 Lesson 3

A block of land bounded by a road has three offsets 23 m, 15 m and 19 m, spaced 20 m apart. What is its approximate area using the Trapezoidal rule?

A 720 m²
B 880 m²
C 1140 m²
D 1440 m²
Answer: A
\(A \approx \tfrac{20}{2}[23+19+2(15)] = 10 \times 72 = 720\) m².

Question 10 Lesson 3

A block of land on a number plane has offsets 1.2 km, 2 km and 1.4 km at x = 0, 6 and 12. Using two applications of the Trapezoidal rule (h = 6), what is the approximate area?

A 99 km²
B 19.8 km²
C 39.6 km²
D 72 km²
Answer: B
\(A \approx \tfrac{6}{2}[1.2 + 1.4 + 2(2)] = 3 \times 6.6 = 19.8\) km².

Question 11 3 marks Lesson 1

A netball court is measured as 30.50 m long, correct to the nearest centimetre.

(a) What is the absolute error, in metres? 1
Absolute error \(= \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{precision} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.01 = 0.005\) m.
(b) What is the lower limit of the court's true length? 1
Lower limit \(= 30.50 - 0.005 = 30.495\) m.
(c) What is the percentage error, correct to 2 d.p.? 1
\(\% \text{error} = \dfrac{0.005}{30.50} \times 100 \approx 0.02\%\).

Question 12 4 marks Lesson 2

A cannon ball made of steel has a diameter of 23 cm. Steel has a density of 8.2 tonnes/m³.

(a) Find the volume of the sphere in cm³, correct to 1 d.p. 2
Radius \(= 11.5\) cm. \(V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi (11.5)^3 = 6370.6\) cm³.
(b) Find the mass of the cannon ball, to the nearest gram. 2
8.2 tonnes/m³ = 8 200 000 g/1 000 000 cm³ = 8.2 g/cm³. Mass \(= 6370.6 \times 8.2 \approx 52\,239\) g.

Question 13 3 marks Lesson 1

A rectangular wooden chopping board is advertised as 17 cm by 25 cm, each side measured to the nearest centimetre.

(a) Calculate the percentage error in the longer side (25 cm). 1
Absolute error \(= 0.5\) cm. \(\% \text{error} = \dfrac{0.5}{25} \times 100 = 2\%\).
(b) Lower limit of the board's area (cm²) 1
Lower bounds: 16.5 cm and 24.5 cm. Area (lower) \(= 16.5 \times 24.5 = 404.25\) cm².
(c) Upper limit of the board's area (cm²) 1
Upper bounds: 17.5 cm and 25.5 cm. Area (upper) \(= 17.5 \times 25.5 = 446.25\) cm².

Question 14 4 marks Lesson 3

A new 200 m long dam has four evenly-spaced cross-sectional areas: 360 m², 300 m², 270 m² and 140 m² measured from the dam wall, tapering to 0 m² at the far end (five values in total, four applications).

(a) Show that the volume of the dam is approximately 44 500 m³. 2
\(h = 200 \div 4 = 50\) m. \(V \approx \tfrac{50}{2}\big[360+0+2(300+270+140)\big] = 25 \times 1780 = 44\,500\) m³.
(b) The dam's catchment area is 2 km². How much rainfall (nearest mm) is needed to fill it? 2
2 km² = 2 000 000 m². \(h = 44\,500 \div 2\,000\,000 = 0.02225\) m \(\approx 22\) mm.

Question 15 3 marks Lesson 2

Amanda uses 80 kilocalories of energy per kilometre while running. She eats a burger containing 2180 kilojoules of energy (1 kilocalorie = 4.184 kilojoules).

(a) Convert the burger's energy to kilocalories, correct to 1 d.p. 1
\(2180 \div 4.184 \approx 521.0\) kcal.
(b) How many kilometres must she run to burn off the burger, to 1 d.p.? 1
\(521.0 \div 80 \approx 6.5\) km.
(c) If she instead cycles, using 40 kcal/km, how far must she cycle to burn off the same burger, to 1 d.p.? 1
\(521.0 \div 40 \approx 13.0\) km — half the energy cost per km means double the distance.
