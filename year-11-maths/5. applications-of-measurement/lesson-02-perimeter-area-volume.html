Perimeter, Area, Surface Area & Volume

Calculate perimeters and areas of plane shapes and composite figures, apply Pythagoras' theorem in measurement contexts, and find the surface area and volume of common 3D solids.

Composite Shapes Pythagoras Surface Area & Volume ~55 min

Key Takeaways

  • Composite shapes: add or subtract simple shapes you already know
  • Pythagoras: \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\) finds a missing side of a right triangle
  • Surface area = total area of every face; volume = space enclosed
  • Always work in one consistent unit before combining measurements

Perimeter & Area of Plane Shapes

These six shapes cover almost every measurement question you'll meet. Learn the formula, then practise spotting these shapes hidden inside composite figures.

Triangle

\(A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh\)

Rectangle

\(A = lb\)

Parallelogram

\(A = bh\)

Trapezium

\(A = \tfrac{1}{2}h(a+b)\)

Circle

\(A = \pi r^2\)

Sector

\(A = \tfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2\)

Worked Example — Composite of a quadrant

Which calculation finds the shaded area shown below (a rectangle 16 cm × 8 cm with a quarter circle of radius 8 cm attached)?

\(A = 16 \times 8 + \dfrac{\pi \times 8^2}{4}\) — the rectangle's area plus the quadrant's area.

Irregular blocks of land

A surveyed paddock or block of land is dissected into triangles, rectangles and trapeziums using perpendicular offset measurements — find each piece's area separately, then add them together.

Pythagoras' theorem in measurement

\[c^2 = a^2 + b^2\]

Worked Example

A shape is made from a quadrant and a right-angled triangle. The triangle has a vertical side of 6 cm and hypotenuse (also the quadrant's radius) of 10 cm. Find the shape's perimeter, to 1 d.p.

Find the radius using Pythagoras: \(r = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = \sqrt{64} = 8\) cm.

Arc length \(= \tfrac{1}{4} \times 2\pi r = \tfrac{1}{4} \times 2\pi \times 8 = 12.57\) cm.

Perimeter \(= 8 + 6 + 10 + 12.57 = 36.6\) cm (1 d.p.)

Pythagoras' theorem — practice in isolation

Worked Example

A ladder leans against a wall. Its foot is 1.5 m from the base of the wall and it reaches 4 m up the wall. How long is the ladder, to 1 d.p.?

\(\text{ladder}^2 = 1.5^2 + 4^2 = 2.25 + 16 = 18.25 \Rightarrow \text{ladder} = \sqrt{18.25} \approx 4.3\) m.

Part B

Surface Area of Prisms, Cylinders & Spheres

Key surface area formulas

Surface area is the total area of every outer face of a solid.

Cylinder: \(SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h\)   ·   Sphere: \(SA = 4\pi r^2\)

For a rectangular prism, find and add the area of each of the 6 faces (opposite faces are equal, so add 3 pairs).

Worked Example

A grain silo is a cylinder (radius 4 m, height 21 m) with a hemisphere on top. Find the total surface area to paint (nearest cm²).

Cylinder curved surface: \(2\pi r h = 2\pi \times 4 \times 21 = 527.79\) m²  +  base is not painted (sits on the ground).

Hemisphere curved surface: \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 4\pi r^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4\pi \times 4^2 = 100.53\) m².

Total: \(527.79 + 100.53 \approx 628\) m².

Worked Example — Composite prism

A garden shed's cross-section is a rectangle (2 m wide, 2 m tall) topped by an isosceles triangle (base 2 m, height 0.8 m). The shed is 3 m long. Find the total surface area to be painted, excluding the base (nearest 0.1 m²).

Two rectangular side walls (trapezium-shaped, but split into rectangle + triangle each): front & back faces \(= 2 \times \left(2 \times 2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\times 2\times0.8\right) = 2\times(4+0.8) = 9.6\) m².

Two long rectangular walls: \(2 \times (2\times3) = 12\) m². Two sloped roof panels: slant length \(=\sqrt{1^2+0.8^2}=1.28\) m, so \(2\times(1.28\times3)=7.68\) m².

Total \(= 9.6 + 12 + 7.68 = 29.3\) m² (1 d.p.) — only visible faces are painted, so the base is excluded.

Volume & Capacity of Solids

Any prism

Volume = area of cross-section × length.

\(V = Ah\)

Cylinder

Circular cross-section prism.

\(V = \pi r^2 h\)

Sphere

All points equidistant from the centre.

\(V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\)

Cone / Pyramid

One-third of the matching prism/cylinder.

\(V = \tfrac{1}{3}Ah\)
