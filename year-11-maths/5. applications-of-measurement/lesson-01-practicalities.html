Part A

Length & Area Units

The metric length ladder

Every step up the ladder multiplies by 10. Every step down divides by 10.

mmcmmkm
1×10×1000×1 000 000

So 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm, and 1 km = 1000 m.

Area units

Squaring the ladder

Because area is a squared quantity, each conversion factor is squared too — so the jump between area units is ×100 per step, not ×10.

mm²cm²m²hakm²
1×100×1 000 000×10 000 000 000×1 000 000 000 000

Key facts: 1 cm² = 100 mm² (10 × 10), 1 m² = 10 000 cm², 1 hectare (ha) = 10 000 m², 1 km² = 100 ha.

Common mistake: converting area with the length factor (×100 instead of ×10 000 for m²→cm²) — always square the length factor.

Part B

Volume & Capacity

Volume units and the capacity bridge

Volume units jump by ×1000 per step (the length factor cubed). Capacity units (mL, L, kL, ML) run alongside them:

mm³cm³m³
1×1000×1 000 000 000
1 cm³ = 1 mL   ·   1 m³ = 1000 L = 1 kL   ·   1 ML = 1 000 000 L

This bridge is exactly what makes Exercise 1 above work: a volume in cubic metres converts directly to kilolitres.

Worked Example

A rectangular prism measures 30 cm × 5 cm × 4 m. What is its volume in cubic centimetres?

Convert every length to the same unit first: \(4\text{ m} = 400\text{ cm}\).

\(V = l \times b \times h = 30 \times 5 \times 400 = 60\,000\text{ cm}^3\)

Worked Example — Scientific notation with significant figures

Write 0.000 000 326 mm in scientific notation.

Move the decimal point until one non-zero digit sits before it: \(0.000\,000\,326 = 3.26 \times 10^{-7}\).

Answer: \(3.26 \times 10^{-7}\) mm (3 significant figures).

Part D

Precision & Measurement Error

Absolute error & percentage error

Every measuring instrument has a limit of precision. The absolute error is half of the smallest unit the instrument can measure:

\[\text{Absolute error} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{precision}\]
\[\% \text{ error} = \dfrac{\text{absolute error}}{\text{measurement}} \times 100\%\]

The true value always lies between (measurement − absolute error) and (measurement + absolute error) — the lower and upper limits.

Worked Example

The length of a window is measured as 2.4 m, to the nearest 0.1 m. Find the percentage error.

Absolute error \(= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.1 = 0.05\) m.

\(\% \text{error} = \dfrac{0.05}{2.4} \times 100 \approx 2.08\%\)
