The Trapezoidal Rule

Estimate the area, volume or capacity of an irregular shape using the Trapezoidal rule — the tool surveyors and engineers use for blocks of land, dams, and cross-sections.

Irregular Areas \(A \approx \tfrac{h}{2}(d_f+d_l)\) ~20 min

Key Takeaways

  • \(h\) is the fixed perpendicular distance between offsets
  • Middle offsets are counted twice (weight 2); first and last once (weight 1)
  • More applications (more offsets) generally give a more accurate estimate
  • The same rule estimates area, volume (with a height/depth) and capacity

Part A

The Trapezoidal Rule

Definition

When a shape's boundary is irregular, measure equally-spaced perpendicular offsets \(d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_n\), a fixed distance \(h\) apart. The Trapezoidal rule approximates the area as:

\[A \approx \dfrac{h}{2}\Big[d_{first} + d_{last} + 2(\text{sum of the middle offsets})\Big]\]
  • \(h\) = the perpendicular distance between each offset (must be equal spacing)
  • \(d_{first}\) and \(d_{last}\) = the two outer offsets (counted once)
  • Every offset in between is counted twice (it is shared by two adjoining trapezia)

Note: the Trapezoidal rule joins offset tops with straight lines, so it usually gives a slightly different value to the true (curved) area — it can over- or under-estimate depending on whether the boundary curves outward or inward between offsets.
