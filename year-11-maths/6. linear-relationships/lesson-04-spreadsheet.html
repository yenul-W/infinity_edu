Key Takeaways

  • A negative gradient models a decreasing relationship

Overview

What is Spreadsheet Modelling?

A spreadsheet model organises data into rows and columns where one column holds the input values (\(x\)) and another holds computed output values (\(y\)). Every output row is calculated using the same formula — exactly like substituting values into a linear equation.

The General Linear Model

Every scenario in this lesson uses a linear equation of the form:

\[y = \text{base} + \text{rate} \times x\]
  • base is the y-intercept — the value of \(y\) when \(x = 0\)
  • rate is the gradient — how much \(y\) changes for each unit increase in \(x\)
  • When the base = 0, the model reduces to direct variation: \(y = kx\)

As you work through the five scenarios below, notice how the equation, the table of values, and the graph are three representations of the same relationship. Changing the rate (gradient) in the slider changes all three simultaneously.

How to use the interactive models: Select a scenario tab (A–E), then drag the slider to change the key parameter. The spreadsheet table and graph update instantly. Use the analysis questions below each model to check your understanding.

Real-World Context — Distance & Time

Planning a road trip from Sydney to Melbourne? The distance you cover depends on how fast you drive. This is a direct variation — doubling your speed doubles the distance for any given time, and if speed = 0 you go nowhere.

Equation: \(d = \text{speed} \times t\) — where \(t\) is time in hours and \(d\) is distance in km.

Analysis Questions

1. At a speed of 80 km/h, how far would you travel in 3 hours?

A 210 km
B 240 km
C 260 km
D 280 km
Answer: B — 240 km
\(d = 80 \times 3 = 240\) km. Set the slider to 80 to confirm the table shows 240 in the row for \(t = 3\).

2. If the graph becomes steeper, what does that tell you about the speed?

A The speed has decreased
B The speed is constant
C The speed has increased
D The distance is zero
Answer: C — The speed has increased
The gradient of the graph equals the speed. A steeper line means a larger gradient, which means a higher speed.

3. This distance-time relationship is an example of direct variation because:

A The graph is a curve
B The line passes through the origin — when \(t = 0\), \(d = 0\)
C The y-intercept is positive
D The speed is always 60 km/h
Answer: B
Direct variation requires the graph to pass through \((0, 0)\). At \(t = 0\) hours, distance = 0 km — you haven't moved yet.

Real-World Context — Sports Performance

A junior basketball player starts with a baseline skill score of 20 (representing basic coordination before structured training). Each hour of deliberate practice improves their score by a constant rate. This gives a linear model with a non-zero y-intercept — unlike direct variation.

Equation: \(\text{skill} = 20 + \text{rate} \times \text{hours}\)

Explore: change the improvement rate

skill = 20 + 3 × hours

Analysis Questions

1. What does the y-intercept of 20 represent in this context?

A The number of practice hours needed
B The starting skill score before any practice begins
C The maximum achievable skill score
D The rate of improvement per hour
Answer: B
The y-intercept is the value of the output when the input is zero. When hours = 0, skill = 20. This is the player's baseline before training starts.

2. With a rate of 5 points/hour, what is the skill score after 12 hours of practice?

A 60
B 70
C 80
D 85
Answer: C — 80
\(\text{skill} = 20 + 5 \times 12 = 20 + 60 = 80\). Set the slider to 5 and check the row for 25 hours — then calculate for 12 hours manually.

3. Why does this relationship NOT show direct variation?

A Because the rate changes at each step
B Because the graph is not a straight line
C Because the y-intercept is 20, not 0
D Because skill score can exceed 100
Answer: C
Direct variation requires the graph to pass through the origin. Here the line starts at \((0, 20)\), so when hours \(= 0\), skill \(= 20 \neq 0\). It is linear, but not proportional.

Real-World Context — Cultural Art Practices

Traditional and Indigenous art practices — from weaving to carving to dot painting — require dedicated time to develop mastery. Students begin with a small amount of foundational knowledge (5%) and build at a constant rate through guided practice. We cap mastery at 100% since you cannot exceed full proficiency.

Equation: \(\text{mastery} = 5 + \text{rate} \times \text{hours}\) (capped at 100%)

Explore: change the learning rate

mastery = 5 + 2 × hours

Analysis Questions

1. At a rate of 4%/hour, how many hours of practice are needed to reach 85% mastery?

A 15 hours
B 18 hours
C 20 hours
D 25 hours
Answer: C — 20 hours
Solve \(85 = 5 + 4 \times h\) → \(80 = 4h\) → \(h = 20\) hours. Set the slider to 4 and observe when the table first reaches 85%.

2. What is the starting mastery level (before any practice)?

A 5%
B 0%
C 10%
D 20%
Answer: A — 5%
The y-intercept is 5. When hours = 0, mastery = 5 + rate × 0 = 5%. This represents foundational knowledge students already have.

3. Which slider value would you use to reach full mastery (100%) in exactly 19 hours?

A 3.5%/h
B 4.5%/h
C 5%/h
D 5.5%/h
Answer: C — 5%/h
Solve \(100 = 5 + r \times 19\) → \(95 = 19r\) → \(r = 5\). A rate of 5%/hour reaches 100% mastery in exactly 19 hours.

Real-World Context — Renewable Energy & Fossil Fuels

As renewable energy is adopted at a constant rate each year, fossil fuel usage decreases. This is a negative gradient relationship — as one quantity increases, the other decreases. The faster the adoption rate, the steeper the decline in fossil fuel usage.

Equation: \(\text{fossil fuel \%} = 100 - \text{rate} \times \text{year}\) (minimum 0%)

Explore: change the annual adoption rate

fossil = 100 − 2 × year
YearFossil Fuel (%)

Live Graph

Analysis Questions

1. At a rate of 3%/year, in how many years will fossil fuel usage fall below 40%?

A 15 years
B 18 years
C 20 years
D 25 years
Answer: C — 20 years
Solve \(40 = 100 - 3 \times t\) → \(3t = 60\) → \(t = 20\) years. Set the slider to 3 and read down the table.

2. The gradient of the fossil fuel graph is negative. What does this mean in context?

A Fossil fuel usage is increasing each year
B Fossil fuel usage is decreasing as renewable energy is adopted
C The relationship is not linear
D Fossil fuel usage starts at zero
Answer: B
A negative gradient means the output (fossil fuel %) decreases as the input (year) increases. Each year, adoption of renewables displaces more fossil fuel usage.

3. At what rate does renewable energy need to be adopted for fossil fuel usage to reach zero in exactly 20 years?

A 3%/year
B 4%/year
C 5%/year
D 6%/year
Answer: C — 5%/year
Solve \(0 = 100 - r \times 20\) → \(r = 100 \div 20 = 5\). Set the slider to 5 and observe that the table reaches 0% at year 20.

Real-World Context — GDP & Economic Growth

A country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) represents the total value of goods and services produced. In a simplified linear model, GDP grows by a constant amount each year. Economists use such models to analyse historical trends and project future growth — though real GDP growth is more complex.

Equation: \(\text{GDP} = 2.0 + \text{rate} \times \text{year}\) ($ trillion, starting from Year 0)

Explore: change the annual GDP growth rate

GDP = 2.0 + 0.20 × year

Live Graph

Analysis Questions

1. At a growth rate of $0.30 trillion/year, what is the projected GDP after 8 years?

A $3.8 trillion
B $4.4 trillion
C $4.8 trillion
D $5.0 trillion
Answer: B — $4.4 trillion
\(\text{GDP} = 2.0 + 0.30 \times 8 = 2.0 + 2.4 = 4.4\). Set the slider to 0.30 and read the Year 8 row.

2. What does the y-intercept of $2.0 trillion represent in this model?

A The GDP at Year 0 — the starting economic output
B The annual growth rate
C The maximum possible GDP
D The number of years in the model
Answer: A
The y-intercept is the value of the output when the input (year) = 0. It represents the country's GDP at the start of the observation period.

3. If the annual growth rate slows from $0.30 to $0.10 trillion/year, what happens to the graph?

A The line shifts down but keeps the same slope
B The line becomes steeper
C The line becomes shallower (less steep) but starts from the same point
D The line becomes horizontal
Answer: C
The growth rate is the gradient. Reducing it from 0.30 to 0.10 makes the slope shallower. The y-intercept ($2.0 trillion) stays the same — the starting GDP doesn't change, only the rate of growth does.
