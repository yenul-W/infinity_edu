Formula and method sheet

Simultaneous equations

  • Graphically: the solution is the point of intersection.
  • Substitution: use when one variable is already isolated.
  • Elimination: use when both are in the form \(ax + by = c\); match coefficients, then add or subtract.
  • Equal gradients + different intercepts → parallel → no solution. Identical equations → infinitely many.

Break-even

\[C = \text{fixed} + (\text{variable per item}) \times n \qquad R = (\text{price}) \times n\] \[P = R - C \qquad\qquad n_{\text{BE}} = \frac{\text{fixed costs}}{\text{price} - \text{variable cost}}\]

The denominator is the contribution per item. Below \(n_{\text{BE}}\) the business makes a loss; above it, a profit.

Exponential

\[y = ka^{x}\]

\(k\) = initial value = \(y\)-intercept. \(a\) = growth/decay factor.
Growth of \(r\%\): \(a = 1 + \frac{r}{100}\).   Decay of \(r\%\): \(a = 1 - \frac{r}{100}\).
Always passes through \((0, k)\); horizontal asymptote \(y = 0\); \(a^{-x} = (1/a)^{x}\).

Quadratic

\[y = ax^{2} + bx + c \qquad\qquad \text{axis of symmetry: } x = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}\]

\(y\)-intercept is \(c\). \(a > 0\) opens up (minimum); \(a < 0\) opens down (maximum). The vertex lies on the axis of symmetry — substitute that \(x\) back in to get \(y\). Constant second differences identify a quadratic table.

Reciprocal

\[y = \frac{k}{x} \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad xy = k\]

Find \(k\) by multiplying any matching pair. Graph is a hyperbola with asymptotes \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\); no intercepts of either kind.

Exam technique

Mistakes that cost marks

MistakeFix
Writing \(a = 0.07\) for 7% growth Growth factor is \(1 + 0.07 = 1.07\). The 1 carries the original amount forward.
Writing \(a = 0.35\) when told "35% remains" That one is 0.35 — read carefully whether the percentage is what is lost or what is left.
Confusing \(y = x^3\) with \(y = 3^x\) Exponential has the variable in the exponent. Power functions have it in the base.
Giving a break-even of 72.9 items Round up to 73. Rounding down leaves the business still at a loss.
Forgetting to define variables in a worded problem "Let \(a\) = number of adult tickets" is often worth a mark on its own.
Stopping at \(x\) and not finding \(y\) A simultaneous-equation answer is a pair. Substitute back every time.
Using \(x = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}\) when there are no \(x\)-intercepts If the parabola does not cross the axis, use \(x = -\frac{b}{2a}\) instead.
Answering "the model is inaccurate" for a limitation question Name the assumption, tie it to the context, and quote the unrealistic number the model produces.
Including \(x = 0\) in the domain of \(y = k/x\) Division by zero is undefined — \(x = 0\) is always excluded.
Leaving off units or a sentence Practical questions want "The maximum height is 45 metres", not just "45".
