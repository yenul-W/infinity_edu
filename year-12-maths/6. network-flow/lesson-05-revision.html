Reference

Terminology and method

The vocabulary

  • Source \(S\) — edges leave, none enter.
  • Sink \(T\) — edges enter, none leave.
  • Flow capacity — the maximum an edge can carry; its weight.
  • Cut — a division separating \(S\) from \(T\) that every path must cross.
  • Capacity of a cut — the sum of the forward-crossing capacities.

The theorem

\[\text{Maximum flow} = \text{Capacity of the minimum cut}\]

The cut checklist

  1. Is it valid? No path from \(S\) to \(T\) may survive.
  2. Does the edge cross the cut? Both endpoints on one side → ignore.
  3. Which direction? Forward (source side → sink side) → count. Backward → count as zero, never subtract.
  4. Add the forward capacities. That is the cut's capacity.

Two facts about upgrades

  • Only edges in a minimum cut constrain the flow. Widening anything else changes nothing.
  • Widening a bottleneck helps only until the next-smallest cut binds, so the gain is usually less than the capacity added.

Constructing an actual flow

Find a path with spare capacity, push as much as its narrowest edge allows, reduce the remaining capacities, and repeat. When the total equals the minimum cut, the flow is maximal — and that equality is your proof.

Exam technique

Mistakes that cost marks

MistakeFix
Taking the minimum of the edges crossing a cut A cut's capacity is the sum of its forward edges. The minimum rule applies to edges in series along a path.
Adding the capacities along a path A path is limited by its narrowest edge.
Subtracting backward edges They count as zero, never as a negative.
Counting edges with both ends on the same side They do not cross the cut, so they are excluded entirely.
Checking only the source and sink cuts The bottleneck may be in the middle. Test every cut.
Using an invalid cut Check that no path from \(S\) to \(T\) survives before using its capacity.
Taking the largest cut as the maximum flow It is the minimum cut — the tightest constraint.
Assuming a widened edge raises the flow by the amount added The next-smallest cut caps the gain. Recalculate all the cuts.
Recommending an upgrade to a non-bottleneck edge Only minimum-cut edges matter. Anything else is wasted.
Answering "not enough capacity" with no numbers State the maximum flow, the demand and the shortfall, and name the bottleneck.
Omitting units in the final answer kL/h, vehicles/hour, parcels/day — always include them.
