Formula sheet

Ratios

In \(a : b\), the total is \(a + b\) parts, and the first quantity is \(\dfrac{a}{a+b}\) of the whole. To divide a quantity: add the parts, divide, multiply. Always check the shares add back to the total.

Scale

\[\text{actual} = \text{drawing} \times n \qquad \text{drawing} = \frac{\text{actual}}{n}\]

For a scale of \(1 : n\). Lengths scale by \(n\), areas by \(n^{2}\), volumes by \(n^{3}\) — but the safe method is to convert the lengths first, then compute the area.

Area

\[A_{\text{trapezium}} = \tfrac12 h(a + b) \qquad A_{\text{circle}} = \pi r^{2}\] \[A \approx \frac{h}{2}\Big[d_f + d_l + 2(d_2 + d_3 + \dots + d_{n-1})\Big]\]

Trapezoidal rule: ends once, middles doubled. \(h = \dfrac{\text{total width}}{\text{number of offsets} - 1}\).

Volume and rates

\[V = Ah \qquad S = \frac{D}{T} \qquad \text{Fuel} = \frac{\text{litres}}{\text{km}} \times 100\] \[E \text{ (kWh)} = P \text{ (kW)} \times t \text{ (h)}\]

Average speed is always total distance ÷ total time, never the mean of the speeds. For fuel, a lower L/100 km is better.

Unit conversions

More marks are lost in this topic to unit errors than to anything else. These are the conversions that appear again and again:

QuantityConversionTypical use
Length\(100\) cm \(= 1\) m; \(1000\) m \(= 1\) km; \(100\,000\) cm \(= 1\) kmMap scales
Area\(10\,000\) cm² \(= 1\) m²; \(10\,000\) m² \(= 1\) haLand areas
Volume\(1\) m³ \(= 1000\) L \(= 1\) kL; \(1000\) kL \(= 1\) MLRainfall, tanks
Rainfall depth\(1\) mm \(= 0.001\) m\(V = Ah\) — divide mm by 1000 first

Exam technique

Mistakes that cost marks

MistakeFix
Simplifying \(50\text{ cm} : 2\text{ m}\) as \(25 : 1\) Convert to the same unit first: \(50 : 200 = 1 : 4\).
Dividing by the number of parts in the ratio instead of the total Add all the parts first, then divide the quantity by that total.
Scaling an area directly by the scale factor Convert the lengths, then multiply. Areas scale by \(n^{2}\), not \(n\).
Doubling the end offsets in the trapezoidal rule Ends once, middles twice. Always.
Using the number of offsets to find \(h\) \(h = \) width ÷ number of strips = width ÷ (offsets − 1).
Leaving rainfall in millimetres in \(V = Ah\) Divide by 1000 to get metres, so the answer comes out in m³.
Writing 2 h 30 min as 2.30 hours It is 2.5 hours. Divide the minutes by 60.
Averaging two speeds to get the average speed Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
Inverting the fuel consumption formula L/100 km \(= \dfrac{\text{litres}}{\text{km}} \times 100\). A bigger number is worse.
Using watts directly in the energy formula Convert to kilowatts first: divide by 1000.
Comparing best buys without a common unit Reduce everything to price per kilogram (or per litre) before comparing.
Not stating that a trapezoidal answer is an estimate Say so — and note that narrower strips improve accuracy.
